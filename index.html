How to test your microphone

Follow these steps to check your microphone and confirm that sound reaches your browser.

  1. Connect your microphone. Plug in your headset or USB microphone, or make sure your device’s built-in microphone is enabled.
  2. Start the test and allow access. Your browser should display a permission prompt. Choose Allow if you want to run the test.
  3. Speak and watch the meter. Talk normally. The level meter and waveform should react to your voice.
  4. Record and play back a sample. Listen for your voice, excessive noise, distortion, or silence.
  5. Stop when finished. Stop the test to release the microphone stream.

Microphone troubleshooting guide

If the test does not work, use the checks below. Menu names differ by browser and operating system.

The browser cannot access my mic

Check the site permission icon beside the address bar and set Microphone to Allow. Reload the page after changing permission. Make sure the site is opened over HTTPS.

The input meter stays at zero

Check the operating system’s sound input settings, choose the intended microphone, disconnect and reconnect the device, and speak closer to it. Close other apps that may exclusively use the mic.

I cannot hear the recording

Turn up your playback volume, check that the correct output device is selected, and try a different browser. A muted microphone or incorrect input can produce a silent recording.

The wrong microphone is selected

Allow access first, then use the input selector if your browser exposes device names. You may also set the default input in your operating system’s sound settings.

My voice sounds quiet or distorted

Move to a quieter room, keep a consistent distance from the mic, reduce input gain if the sound clips, and inspect any headset mute switch or inline controls.

The test is not supported

Update your browser and check that microphone access is not blocked by device policy, private browsing settings, parental controls, or workplace restrictions.

Understanding your microphone level

The meter is a quick activity indicator, not a calibrated sound-pressure measurement. A moving meter means the browser is receiving audio, but it does not guarantee that a meeting app or recording program is configured correctly.

No movement

The browser may not have permission, the wrong input may be selected, or the microphone may be muted or disconnected.

Small movement

Try speaking closer to the microphone and check input gain. Background noise and mic placement affect the reading.

Strong movement

A strong response usually means sound is reaching the browser. Listen to your sample to judge clarity and distortion.

Frequently asked questions

Is this microphone test free?

Yes. The microphone test on this page is free to use in a compatible browser.

Do I need to install anything?

No installation is required. The browser must support microphone access and audio recording features.

Does this website upload my microphone recording?

The live meter is processed in your browser, and the sample is created for on-page playback. This page does not send the sample to a Mic Test Pro server. Other browser extensions or device software are outside this page’s control.

Why does the browser ask for permission?

Browsers require permission before a website can access a microphone. Denying permission prevents the test from starting.

Can I test my microphone on a phone?

Usually yes, in a current mobile browser. Connect your headset first and allow microphone access. Device and browser support can vary.

Does a successful test mean my microphone works in Zoom or another app?

Not necessarily. Each app has its own input selection, permission settings, and audio controls. Check those settings separately if the browser test works but an app does not.
